Where Things Stand Today

A code review found 33 issues to resolve

The core is built, but it isn't ready for internal use yet. A review of the current Base44 build surfaced 33 concrete issues across three categories, several of them serious enough to block daily use or expose data. This is exactly the work Runway A closes out first.

33 issues found
19Broken API / DB calls

RLS gaps, invalid enums, wrong SDK methods, price mismatches.

5Security issues MOST URGENT

Open email relay, unauth AI, cross-tenant reads of confidential data.

9Refactors & cleanup

Missing error handling, non-transactional writes, version drift.

The hard part is built; the finishing isn't. Most of the 33 are broken API/DB calls, unglamorous but fixable. The five security issues are the ones that matter most: an open email relay, an unauthenticated AI endpoint, and confidential medical data readable across tenants. These must be closed before LFV can rely on Elfve for internal daily use.

The Platform Decision · Risk

Beyond the numbers

The real question is whether Base44 can carry a product you sell, and it wasn't built to. Four risks a price tag can't show:

Possible blocker

May not be feasible

Multi-tenant isolation, billing and sign-up may simply not be buildable on Base44.

High risk

No safety net

No staging or testing, so a routine fix can break production and spike cost.

Structural

The roadmap stalls

No one owns the code, so features stop shipping and the product can't grow.

Long-term

No lasting confidence

A rented platform you can't fully control is hard to stand behind, or sell to clients, for the long run.

Owning it instead

Own the code & security Flat cost, a team that ships Upfront migration cost Relies on a partner team

Our read: owning costs more up front, but it's what makes a sellable, evolving product possible at all.

How We'll Work Together

The relationship, for each option

Each runway is a different kind of working relationship, not just a different price.
Here's what engaging us actually feels like day to day under A, B, and C.

A · Fix

a defined piece of work

Shape: one fixed-scope project with a clear finish line.

Contact: kickoff, a mid-point check, and handoff.

You get: a stabilized app and a clean issues list closed out.

Ends: on delivery, no ongoing commitment.

B · Build as you go

an ongoing partnership

Shape: a standing monthly team, like a fractional dev shop.

Contact: weekly demos, a monthly gate where you re-prioritize.

You get: steady shipped progress and full say over what's next.

Ends: whenever you choose, pause or accelerate anytime.

C · Rapid Development

a committed build partnership

Shape: a focused project with a shared launch goal & date.

Contact: weekly demos, milestone reviews, launch planning.

You get: the fastest route to a sellable product, plus UX & market input.

Ends: at launch, optional support retainer after.

Same team, different intensity. A is a favor to your current tool; B is a partner walking with you to product; C is a partner sprinting with you to a launch date. You can move from one to the next as your confidence grows.
